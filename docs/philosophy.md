# Preservation Philosophy

*This page describes how the repository treats the artifact. It does not
interpret the Proclamation. The text speaks for itself and is reproduced
verbatim elsewhere.*

## Constitutional, not living

The Proclamation is handled as a **constitutional artifact** — an instrument
whose value depends on its wording remaining fixed and its provenance remaining
verifiable. It is therefore not edited, modernised, or paraphrased.

## Kaivalyik Immutabilis as a functional rule

The motto is treated here as an **operational preservation rule**, not merely a
sentiment: the canonical parchment is never overwritten; integrity is fixed by
cryptographic hash; existence-in-state is fixed by decentralised timestamp; and
citation is fixed by DOI. Together these make the artifact resistant to silent
alteration by any single party — the practical meaning of "neither stolen by
thieves, nor usurped by sceptres" in an archival system.

## Balance of Interpretation

The phrase **"Balance of Interpretation"** appears in the canonical text. This
repository takes no position on its meaning and adds none; it preserves the
phrase exactly as written and leaves interpretation to readers and scholars.
